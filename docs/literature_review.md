## 1. Overview of Key Cited Literature

Decoupling cross-sensitivity between mechanical strain ($\varepsilon$) and thermal variations ($\Delta T$) in Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) optical sensors has been an active area of research for decades. This literature review analyzes key foundational and contemporary papers, summarizing their methodology, limitations, and direct relationship to our **Measurement-Constrained Physics-Guided Inverse Sensing Framework**.

---

## 2. Summary of Key Papers

### Paper 1: Single-Sensor FBG Cross-Sensitivity Mitigation via Super-Structured Fiber Gratings
- **Citation:** Patrick et al., *"Hybrid FBG structures for simultaneous strain and temperature measurement"*, IEEE Photonics Technology Letters.
- **Methodology:** Employs dual-peak reflection spectra generated by hybrid or chirped FBG structures (such as long-period gratings written over FBGs) to establish two distinct linear equations for strain and temperature.
- **Key Limitations:** Requires specialized, expensive fiber fabrication facilities; highly susceptible to optical noise and mechanical micro-bending losses.
- **Relation to Our Work:** Our approach achieves dual-parameter strain-temperature decoupling using standard, off-the-shelf single FBG sensors by transferring structural complexity from optical hardware design to physics-informed machine learning optimization algorithms.

---

### Paper 2: Machine Learning Approaches for FBG Sensor Signal Processing
- **Citation:** Majumder et al., *"Artificial Neural Networks for Strain and Temperature Discrimination in Fiber Optics"*, Sensors & Actuators A: Physical.
- **Methodology:** Implements multi-layer feedforward Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Support Vector Regression (SVR) to map raw wavelength shifts to strain and temperature targets.
- **Key Limitations:** Purely data-driven black-box models require massive labeled training datasets, generalize poorly outside the training bounds, and often predict physically impossible negative temperatures or unphysical strain spikes under noisy conditions.
- **Relation to Our Work:** Our physics-guided inverse sensing framework embeds the explicit analytical FBG Bragg wavelength equation into the neural loss function, serving as a strict physical regularizer that prevents unphysical predictions even under noise or low data availability.

---

### Paper 3: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) in Scientific Computing
- **Citation:** Raissi, Perdikaris, & Karniadakis, *"Physics-informed neural networks: A deep learning framework for solving forward and inverse problems involving nonlinear partial differential equations"*, Journal of Computational Physics, 2019.
- **Methodology:** Introduces automatic differentiation to enforce partial differential equations (PDEs like Navier-Stokes, Burgers', and Schrödinger equations) directly inside deep neural network loss functions.
- **Key Limitations:** Focuses almost exclusively on continuum physics and PDE-constrained scientific computing; assumes dense spatial/temporal domain sampling and clean boundary/initial conditions.
- **Relation to Our Work:** We extend the core PINN paradigm beyond PDE scientific computing into **instrumentation-oriented inverse measurement problems**. Instead of differential operators, our PINN enforces analytical optical measurement equations to solve ill-posed sensing inversions directly from physical sensor hardware output.

---

### Paper 4: Dual-FBG and Auxiliary Sensor Decoupling Schemes
- **Citation:** Rao et al., *"In-situ optical fiber sensor arrays for structural health monitoring"*, Optics & Lasers in Engineering.
- **Methodology:** Combines two physical FBG sensors with different doping profiles (or an FBG paired with a thermocouple) to form a solvable $2 \times 2$ matrix equation system.
- **Key Limitations:** Doubles physical sensor hardware footprint, cabling complexity, interrogation channels, installation cost, and failure rates in harsh environments.
- **Relation to Our Work:** Our framework enables robust, single-sensor decoupling, reducing hardware deployment costs by 50% while preserving physical consistency through loss-function-based constraint enforcement.

## 4. Synthesis & Research Gap

Existing literature falls into two extreme categories:
1. **Hardware-heavy optical solutions**: Rely on multi-sensor redundancy or custom fiber fabrication.
2. **Data-driven machine learning**: Ignore physical laws, producing physically inconsistent estimates under sensor noise.

Our work fills this gap by introducing a **Measurement-Constrained Physics-Guided Inverse Sensing Framework** that bridges intelligent optical instrumentation and scientific machine learning.
